# 2026-11-12: Envelope, measured and validated (issue #21)

Envelope is the Level element "the level of a sound followed over a few milliseconds". The third edition of the table predicted it at τ ≈ 10 ms (2 to 50 ms, Z ≈ 8.3) but had no measurement. This study builds a reading of the envelope with our own maths, measures its errors on signals whose envelope is known exactly, and decides whether Envelope counts as checked.

**Verdict.** Envelope is a **checked element for tonal sounds**, under stated conditions. It is **not checked for noise-like sounds at its typical time**. Four of the five criteria fixed before the runs are met on held-out data. The fifth, time scaling within 5%, is met for tones but **not for a noise carrier that is compressed**. The reading is adopted into `pts/` as `amplitude_envelope()`.

## What was measured

**Signals:** a(t) · c(t) + n(t), where a(t), the true envelope, is known exactly.

| Shape | Characteristic time T |
|---|---|
| rise (raised cosine) | 10–90% rise time |
| decay (exponential) | 90–10% fall time |
| burst (Gaussian) | full width at half maximum |
| constant | (no change; used for false change and linearity) |

**Carriers c(t):** a sine and a harmonic tone (12 harmonics, 1/k), and white noise. **Additive noise n(t):** none, 30, 20 or 10 dB SNR.

**Estimators:** the moving RMS, and the magnitude of the analytic signal (FFT, negative frequencies zeroed). Both are smoothed by a zero-phase Hann window w. Times are read after the noise floor is removed in power: √(e² − floor²).

**Errors reported:**
- the relative error of T;
- the shape error (RMS of the peak-normalised difference over the event);
- the false change of a constant level (max/min in dB);
- linearity (doubling the amplitude must add 6.02 dB);
- under tape-speed scaling by k, the ratio T_k / T_1 against k, and ΔZ against log₂ k.

### What went wrong on the way

1. **The noise floor was removed in amplitude (v1).** v1 subtracted the floor as e − floor. Noise adds in power, so in additive noise every time came out short, by 9 to 23% at 20 dB, and only 1 of 88 region cells passed at 20 dB. This was an implementation error, not a limit of the method. v2 subtracts in power. v1 can still be run (`FLOOR = "amplitude"`).
2. **The scaling run crashed.** Analysis margins fixed in seconds were longer than a signal compressed eight times. The margins now scale with the tape speed; nothing changes at speed 1.
3. **The development scaling test was outside the region.** It used a 2 ms window on a 220 Hz carrier (w·f0 = 0.44), so it did not test H4. The held-out scaling was built inside the region.
4. **The run log missed untracked files.** Grid v1 ran on an uncommitted `envelope.py`, which `git diff` does not see. `runlog.py` now also hashes every `.py` file of the experiment.

## Results

### The valid region (fixed on development, confirmed on held-out)

A reading of a change lasting T, with window w, is valid when:
- **tonal carrier (sine, harmonic tone), all of:**
  - w ≤ T/4;
  - w · f0 ≥ 2 (at least two carrier periods in the window);
  - if noise is present: SNR ≥ 20 dB, w ≥ T/50 and w ≥ 2 ms.

  Together these mean the change must last **at least 8 carrier periods**.
- **noise carrier:** 10 ms ≤ w ≤ T/4, so only changes of **40 ms and longer**, and SNR ≥ 20 dB.
- **SNR below 20 dB:** outside the region.

### Inside the region, held out

| Estimator | Region cells passing | Cases | Median \|time error\| | 95th percentile | Worst | Median shape error | Worst shape error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| analytic | **279 of 279** | 2511 | **0.32%** | 3.92% | 12.9% | 0.003 | 0.035 |
| moving RMS | 279 of 279 | 2511 | 0.38% | 3.56% | 12.7% | 0.003 | 0.031 |

- **Constant level:** a tonal level shows at most **0.61 dB** of false change. A noise carrier shows up to 1.69 dB, which is its own level fluctuation, not an error of the reading.
- **Linearity:** doubling the amplitude adds 6.0206 dB in every case.

### Where it depends on pitch, noise and the window

| Condition (outside the region, held out, analytic) | Cells | Passing |
|---|---|---|
| fewer than 2 carrier periods in the window | 300 | 51% |
| window longer than T/4 | 504 | 34% |
| noise carrier, window under 10 ms or over T/4 | 129 | 21% |
| SNR 10 dB | 432 | 20% |
| tonal, noisy, with w < T/50 or w < 2 ms | 84 | 92% |

- **Pitch.** A change cannot be read faster than the carrier allows. On development, with the best window, the shortest readable change scales as about 1/f0: 10 ms at 55 Hz (sine), 5 ms at 220 Hz, 1 ms at 880 Hz and above.
  - A low harmonic tone (55 Hz) was not readable at all within the windows tested, because its harmonics beat at f0.
  - At Envelope's typical τ of 10 ms, a tone needs f0 ≥ about 800 Hz.
  - The table's whole range down to 2 ms needs f0 ≥ about 4 kHz.
- **Noise.**
  - Additive noise makes the level fluctuate. On a slow change a short window turns that fluctuation into a large error of time, hence w ≥ T/50.
  - At 10 dB most cells fail.
  - A noise carrier fluctuates by itself, so it needs a long window.
- **Window.** Too long a window smears the change (w ≤ T/4); too short a window lets the carrier through (w · f0 ≥ 2). The analytic estimator tolerates a short window on a pure sine, because a sine has no ripple in its analytic magnitude. On harmonic tones and noise it has no such advantage. It is preferred for that reason, and because the two estimators are otherwise equal inside the region.

### Time scaling and the Level column

Tape-speed scaling by k (the carrier moves too), with the window scaled by k, held out:

| Carrier | k = 1/4 | 1/2 | 2 | 4 | Worst \|ΔZ − log₂ k\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sine | 0.02% | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.000 |
| harmonic | 0.55% | 0.08% | 0.08% | 0.12% | 0.008 |
| noise | **7.16%** | **6.44%** | 0.21% | 0.21% | 0.107 |

Worst ratio error per k.

- **Tones:** the measured time changes by exactly k, and Z moves by log₂ k. Tape speed keeps both conditions of the region (w ≤ T/4 and w · f0 ≥ 2), because w, T and 1/f0 all scale together. A further 20 seeds of the harmonic case at k = 1/4 gave a median of 0.09% and a maximum of 1.97%.
- **Rows:** the element moves between rows (Tone, Event, Beat) as Z moves. This is the periodic law of the table: the same question at another time scale. **It stays a Level element:** the reading is still an amplitude, linear, with the same normalised shape.
- **Noise, compressed: the criterion is not met.** A compressed white noise keeps its full bandwidth while the scaled window shrinks below 10 ms, which takes the case outside the region. The readability of a noise envelope depends on the absolute bandwidth × window product, and tape speed changes it. For a stretched noise, the criterion holds (0.21%).
- **The envelope alone (carrier fixed) has a lower limit.** A change shorter than about 8 carrier periods can no longer be separated from the carrier. Below that limit, a fast change of level is part of the waveform (an attack or a click), not an envelope. This is where the Level column ends at short times for a given pitch.

## Criteria, as fixed before the runs

| Criterion | Held-out result |
|---|---|
| time error: median ≤ 10%, 95th percentile ≤ 20% in every region cell | **met** (279 of 279) |
| shape error ≤ 0.05 | **met** (worst 0.035) |
| constant tonal level: false change < 1 dB | **met** (worst 0.61 dB) |
| doubling the amplitude: 6.02 ± 0.1 dB | **met** (exact) |
| tape-speed factor within 5% | **met for tones** (worst 0.55%); **not met for a compressed noise carrier** (up to 7.2%) |
| the region covers the table's τ range, 2 to 50 ms, for tones above some f0 | **met for f0 ≥ about 4 kHz**; at the typical 10 ms, f0 ≥ about 800 Hz |

## Use in the project

- **`pts.amplitude_envelope(x, sr, window_s)`:** the analytic estimator, v2. Its docstring states the valid region. Tests are in `tests/test_envelope.py`: ground truth, a control, linearity, scaling, equality with this study's code, a known limit outside the region, and degenerate input.
- **The table (third edition):** Envelope is marked ✓ for Z 5.62 to 13.26 (1.5 to 300 ms), with the conditions above.
- **Not adopted:** a check for noise carriers at short times. Their own fluctuation is a separate property, closer to Irregularity than to Level.

## Limits

- **Synthetic signals only.** Real instruments have several partials with their own envelopes, vibrato, and room reverberation. A check on real notes with known onsets is the next step.
- **Smooth shapes only.** Rises are raised cosines and decays are exponential. Other shapes, such as linear-in-dB ramps or two-stage attacks, are untested.
- **One harmonic recipe** (12 harmonics, 1/k). Inharmonic sounds and sounds with strong beating are untested.
- **The windows were limited to 20 and 30 ms.** Low harmonic tones were therefore unreadable, not shown to be impossible.
