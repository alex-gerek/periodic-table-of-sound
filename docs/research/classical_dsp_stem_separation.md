# Grid-locked kernel modelling beats PHARMAKON's current splitter

The strongest purely classical way to split a stereo mix into vocals, drums, bass and other is not a single algorithm. It is a **kernel-additive pipeline**: each stem gets its own simple, explainable model of its power spectrum, and a **stereo multichannel Wiener filter** combines those models so the stems always sum back to the mix. For PHARMAKON's loop-based 4/4 material, four models do the work: a bar-synchronous repetition model for the accompaniment, driven by the tempo grid the product already has; a short-window median HPSS with a residual class for drums; a long-window, kick-gated bass model with an F0 comb; and an F0-plus-voice-gate harmonic model for vocals. Every part of this is published, cheap (seconds to tens of seconds on a CPU) and buildable from scratch in NumPy/SciPy. It also fixes three of the four measured defects by construction: the -17.5 dB reconstruction error, the zero vocal side share and the kick in the bass stem. Expectations need to be honest. Every model-based method in the SiSEC 2016 and 2018 evaluations finished below every neural entry, "by a large margin" in the organisers' words ([Stöter et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06267)). A well-built classical system on easy, repetitive material should land somewhere between a few dB above today's scores and roughly half of HT Demucs' ~9 dB average ([Demucs README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). The main IP exposure is that repetition, the cue that suits this material best, is the subject of **Northwestern's active REPET patent US 9,093,056**, with an adjusted expiry of 2033 ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9093056B2)). That needs a freedom-to-operate review or a licence before anything ships. This report is not legal advice.

## Why the current splitter scores -11.7 dB on vocals

The current chain combines median-filter HPSS, a mid/side panning mask and a fixed 130 Hz bass lowpass. Each measured failure traces back to a mechanism. **Stems do not sum to the mix** (-17.5 dB reconstruction error) because the masks come from separate decisions and are not normalised against each other. Soft masks that sum to one per time-frequency bin, applied to the mixture STFT, reconstruct the mixture exactly. Among the SiSEC 2018 oracles, the squared-error-optimal soft filters (IRM2 and the multichannel Wiener filter) gave "the best overall performance", while binary masks won only on the ISR metric ([Stöter et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06267)). **Vocals have no stereo** (side share 0 against 0.26 in the reference) because the vocal comes from the mid signal. Applying one real-valued mask to the L and R STFTs separately keeps the stereo image, and a full-rank 2×2 spatial covariance per source also captures reverb and width that a single panning vector cannot (Duong, Vincent, Gribonval, IEEE TASLP 18(7), 2010, as described in [Rafii et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08300)). **The kick ends up in the bass stem** (-26 dB left in drums) because a fixed lowpass assigns by frequency, not by sound type. **Vocals and drums score about -12 dB** because HPSS separates spectral *shapes* (horizontal ridges versus vertical lines), not instruments. A sung note with vibrato counts as "harmonic" and so does a synth pad, and the panning mask sends everything centred to vocals, including kick, snare and bass.

Spatial cues alone yield a "centre stem", not a vocal stem. Commercial mixes do put the lead vocal in the centre and let it dominate ([Rafii et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08300)), but kick, snare and bass usually sit there too. The fix is not a better panning mask. It is to give each stem its own model and let a Wiener filter arbitrate between them.

## Six classical families, one unifying framework

The literature falls into six method families. Kernel Additive Modelling (KAM) shows that most of them are special cases of one estimator.

| Family | Core math | What it separates | Reported quality and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Median-filter HPSS** (FitzGerald 2010) and **H/P/Residual** (Driedger, Müller, Disch 2014) | H = time-median of \|X\|, P = frequency-median; mask M_H = H^p/(H^p+P^p). The HPR variant uses binary masks with a separation factor β > 1 and sends ambiguous bins to a residual R = 1 − M_H − M_P | Stable vs transient, not instruments | HPSS/HPLP entries were in the SiSEC 2018 model-based group, behind DNNs "by a large margin" ([Stöter et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06267)); linear time, about 1 s per track |
| **Repetition: REPET, adaptive REPET, REPET-SIM** (Rafii & Pardo 2012–13) | Beat spectrum = mean row autocorrelation of the power spectrogram; background = element-wise median of period-length segments, min'd with the mixture; soft mask; 100 Hz high-pass on voice | Repeating accompaniment vs non-repeating lead | **0.016 s of compute per audio second**, 0.212 s/s windowed on full tracks, against 3.9–7.6 s/s for Durrieu's NMF; competitive with the state of the art at the time ([Rafii & Pardo 2013](https://interactiveaudiolab.github.io/assets/papers/Rafii-Pardo%20-%20REpeating%20Pattern%20Extraction%20Technique%20(REPET)%20A%20Simple%20Method%20for%20Music-Voice%20Separation%20-%20TALSP%202013.pdf)) |

| **Pitch-informed harmonic masking** (MELODIA, SHS + Viterbi) | Salience by harmonic summation; F0 path by Viterbi; comb mask at h·f0 | Predominant melodic lead | **MELO "stands above" the other model-based methods for vocals** in SiSEC 2018 ([Stöter et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06267)) |

**KAM** (Liutkus, FitzGerald, Rafii, Pardo, Daudet, IEEE TSP 2014) assumes that "a source at a TF location can be modeled using its values at other locations through a specified kernel", where the kernel can encode "periodicity, self-similarity, stability over time or frequency" ([Rafii et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08300)). Each iteration has two steps. First, the stereo Wiener filter estimates each source image. Second, each source's PSD v_j(f,t) is replaced by a **median of its own estimate over that source's kernel**. A vertical kernel gives HPSS's percussive model, a horizontal kernel gives the harmonic model, a comb at the period gives REPET, and k-nearest-neighbour frames give REPET-SIM. On DSD100 full tracks, the repetition-exploiting KAM and RAF entries "behave much better for full-length tracks" than plain RPCA, because "vocals are rarely as repetitive as the accompaniment" ([Rafii et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08300)). Comparing about 372 ms of temporal context instead of single frames added about **0.5 dB** of vocal and accompaniment SDR over single-frame KAM ([Fano Yela et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1702.02130)). This framework is the right backbone for PHARMAKON. It turns the separator into a set of swappable per-stem models with one principled, mixture-consistent reconstruction step, and it is a natural place for the product's grid and onset knowledge.

Exact per-method SDR medians from SiSEC 2016 and 2018 exist only as box plots in the papers. The underlying data live in the public results dataframes ([sisec-mus-results](https://github.com/faroit/sisec-mus-results), [sigsep-mus-2018](https://github.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-2018)), which this research did not download. Pulling them is the cheapest way to get exact classical-versus-neural numbers per stem.

## Telling a singer from a synth lead takes four cues, not one

No single signal feature separates voice from a similar-timbre lead. **Vibrato is the most cited cue and the least reliable.** Sustained sung notes vibrate at about **5.5–6.7 Hz** with extents of about **±34 to ±123 cents** (mean ±71) ([IJRCS, Mann](https://acda-publications.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/IJRCS/IJRCS5-1mann.pdf)). Choral and straight-tone styles drop to about 10 cents ([IJRCS, Hinkley](https://acda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/IJRCSVol9Hinkley.pdf)), and guitars, saxophones and synths with vibrato fall in the same range. Re-implemented detectors on the Jamendo corpus show the ceiling. Lehner's classical feature detector reached **87.9% accuracy**, slightly above a CNN at 86.8%, yet on one song they misclassified guitar as voice **62.5%** and 56.5% of the time. Synthetic vibrato at singer-like rate and extent was "often misclassified" ([Schlüter et al. review](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.01180)). Classical features are therefore not inferior to learned ones for this decision, but neither is reliable alone.

The discriminating evidence is a combination, scored per pitch-contour segment rather than per frame. **First, formant motion at constant pitch.** A singer changes vowels every syllable while holding a note; a guitar or synth keeps a fixed envelope unless it is automated. Lehner's "Vocal Variance", the variance of MFCCs 1–5 over 11 frames ([Lehner et al. 2015](https://new.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2015/papers/1570097385.pdf)), measures this directly. In RPCA output, formant structures appear in the sparse part exactly where vocals are active ([Huang et al.](https://posenhuang.github.io/papers/RPCA_Separation_ICASSP2012.pdf)). **Second, unvoiced bursts at segment boundaries.** Consonants and sibilants show up as high-flatness energy above roughly 4–5 kHz next to voiced F0 segments. No published numeric threshold was found, so this cue must be tuned on PHARMAKON's own ground-truth stems. **Third, F0-contour shape.** Vibrato rate and extent come from the detrended cents contour c(t) = 1200·log₂(f₀/f_ref), using the 4–8 Hz peak of its spectrum. Glides between notes and the contour-level characteristics MELODIA uses for voicing and melody selection add to this ([Salamon & Gómez 2012](https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/02_teaching/2024s_sarntal/01_group_F0/2012_SalamonG_Melodia_TALSP.pdf)). Regnier and Peeters' detector, which requires both frequency modulation and amplitude modulation (tremolo) on tracked partials, is the reference design ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00662312)). **Fourth, context: non-repetition and placement.** Lead vocals rarely repeat bar for bar the way a loop does, and they sit near panning index ψ = (|L|−|R|)/(|L|+|R|) ≈ 0 with high inter-channel coherence.

For AI-generated material, vibrato should get a *lower* weight than classical detectors give it. One blog claims Suno vocals have an unnaturally rigid F0 ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/arthurvalle1_a2586dd2b4bc/how-to-tell-if-a-song-was-made-by-ai-1mbg)), but no peer-reviewed measurement supports this. Codec artifacts can also lower harmonicity in the vocal band. Some cases will stay unresolved whatever the weighting: a synth doubling the vocal melody in unison shares pitch, panning and non-repetition, which leaves only the envelope and consonant cues. Stacked harmonies defeat a single-F0 comb. Vocal chops used as loops will, correctly in a repetition sense, land in "other".

## The recommended pipeline: grid-synchronous KAM with a stereo Wiener filter

The combination below is a design inference from the cited components, not a published system. It is the strongest fit for 2–5 minute looped 4/4 tracks within a 30 s CPU budget.

**Front end and bass/kick low band.** Compute STFTs at three resolutions: about 512–1024 samples for drums, 4096 for vocals and other, and 16384–32768 below about 250 Hz. At 44.1 kHz the longest windows give 1.3–2.7 Hz bins, enough to resolve 40–80 Hz bass partials. Express every median length in seconds and Hz rather than bins so that 44.1 and 48 kHz behave identically. In the low band, replace the fixed 130 Hz lowpass with three cooperating models. A long-window HPSS uses a time median of 200 ms or more, so sustained bass reads as harmonic and a 50–150 ms kick body does not. The **existing kick-onset detector** adds a decaying percussive bias for about 10–80 ms after each onset. A bass F0 track (harmonic summation over 30–300 Hz, Viterbi-smoothed) turns into a comb-shaped bass PSD at h·f₀, h = 1..8. Driedger's residual class (β ≈ 2–3) gives ambiguous attack and decay energy somewhere to go instead of forcing it into H or P ([Driedger et al. 2014](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/content/05-fau/professor/00-mueller/03-publications/2014_DriedgerMuellerDisch_SeparationHPR_ISMIR.pdf)). Tonal 808 sub-kicks remain an irreducible case: pitch alone cannot tell them from bass, and only onset timing and decay shape can.

**Drums.** Use short-window median HPSS with the residual class to get a clean transient layer. Then exploit the grid: drum patterns in loop music repeat bar for bar, so the median across bar-aligned repetitions of the percussive layer separates the steady kit from incidental transients such as vocal plosives and synth plucks. The REPET authors themselves note that drums repeat and end up in the "background" ([Rafii & Pardo 2013](https://interactiveaudiolab.github.io/assets/papers/Rafii-Pardo%20-%20REpeating%20Pattern%20Extraction%20Technique%20(REPET)%20A%20Simple%20Method%20for%20Music-Voice%20Separation%20-%20TALSP%202013.pdf)). That is a weakness for vocal extraction and an asset for drum extraction.

**Vocals.** The accompaniment model is the median across bars at the same position within the section, with the period taken from PHARMAKON's tempo/downbeat grid instead of a beat spectrum, and nearest-bar similarity for sections that change. Loop-based electronic music is close to the ideal case for this assumption ([Rafii et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08300)), although no source quantifies repetition-method gains by genre. On top of that, follow the strongest published classical vocal recipe: repetition first, then an F0-driven harmonic mask. Ikemiya, Itoyama and Yoshii's RPCA-plus-harmonic-mask system beat both of its components alone, and its entries had the best mean vocal NSDR in MIREX 2014. Their 44.1 kHz settings were a 4096/441 STFT, 20 harmonics with weights 0.86^(n−1), an 80–720 Hz F0 range, a 150-cent Viterbi transition and a 70 Hz mask width ([Ikemiya et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.00192)). Rafii, Duan and Pardo independently found that combining rhythm-based and pitch-based masks beats either alone ([Rafii et al. 2014](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~zra446/doc/Rafii-Duan-Pardo%20-%20Combining%20Rhythm-Based%20and%20Pitch-Based%20Methods%20for%20Background%20and%20Melody%20Separation%20-%20TALSP%202014.pdf)). The two cues fail in complementary ways: repetition misses non-repeating instruments, and pitch misses consonants and grabs instrumental leads. The four-cue voice gate decides whether each contour segment is vocal; a separate centre-weighted, non-repeating high-frequency model catches sibilants; and a 100 Hz high-pass keeps bass out ([Rafii & Pardo 2013](https://interactiveaudiolab.github.io/assets/papers/Rafii-Pardo%20-%20REpeating%20Pattern%20Extraction%20Technique%20(REPET)%20A%20Simple%20Method%20for%20Music-Voice%20Separation%20-%20TALSP%202013.pdf)). Spatial position enters as a **soft prior** on the vocal PSD, not a hard mid-only extraction, so stereo backing vocals and reverb keep their width.

**Reconstruction.** Feed the four PSDs into 2–3 KAM backfitting iterations. Each iteration applies the stereo MWF with per-source full-rank 2×2 spatial covariances, re-estimates each PSD by its kernel median, and updates the covariances. "Other" gets a self-similar kernel and absorbs everything the other models reject. Because the Wiener gains sum to identity in every bin, **the four stems sum to the mix to numerical precision**, which removes the -17.5 dB defect outright. Optionally add one or two consistency iterations of the MISI kind (multiple-input Griffin-Lim that preserves the sum), but their gains on music benchmarks were not verified.

**Runtime.** A 4-minute stereo track at hop 1024 has about 21 million bins per channel. Median filters, bar medians, SHS salience and comb masks are all roughly linear in bins and take seconds in vectorised NumPy. The expensive pieces are the 32k-sample low-band STFT, which is small because it only covers below 250 Hz, and the KAM iterations at about one Wiener pass plus one median per source per iteration. The total should land well under 30 s. Avoid full RPCA via iterative SVD and full T×T similarity matrices. Bar-aligned medians from the known grid are both cheaper and better matched to the material. These runtimes are engineering estimates, not measured figures.

**Expected quality.** The available benchmarks set the bounds. On DSD100, the one directly verified classical vocal figure is **1.3 dB SDR (OZE)**, against 4.1–5.3 dB for 2016 DNNs ([Wierstorf et al. 2017](https://hagenw.github.io/pubs/wierstorf_et_al-2017-evaluation_of_source_separation_for_remixing.pdf)). Modern neural systems average **5.3 dB (Open-Unmix), 7.7 dB (Hybrid Demucs) and about 9.0 dB (HT Demucs)** across the four MUSDB18-HQ stems, and Demucs runs at about 1.5× real time on a CPU ([Demucs README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). Two things should help PHARMAKON relative to DSD100: the near-literal repetition of AI loop music and the externally supplied grid and kick onsets. On that basis, a reasonable target is to move vocals and drums from about -12 dB into roughly the **0 to +4 dB SI-SDR** range, bass to about **+3 to +6 dB**, and other modestly above today's +1.3 dB. A stem in that band is usable for mastering-grade stem processing but clearly below HT Demucs. This range is an inference with wide uncertainty. Note also that SI-SDR on one vocal-heavy Suno export is not comparable to museval SDR on MUSDB18. The decisive internal benchmark costs nothing to build: compute the **oracle IRM2/MWF from the same STFTs on the ground-truth stems**. That ceiling bounds what any mask-based pipeline at those resolutions can reach. Track each stage's gain against it on a Pareto basis: defects must go down while the mix character stays intact.

**REPET is the crux.** Repetition is the most valuable cue for loop-based music, and Northwestern holds an active US patent whose specification describes both the periodic median model and the similarity-matrix variant. Two engineering choices *might* differ from its claims: taking the period from an external tempo grid instead of a beat spectrum, and embedding the repetition kernel inside KAM backfitting. Neither is a safe design-around until counsel has compared them with the full claim set, which this research did not retrieve. Two facts are worth checking right away. First, the 11.5-year maintenance fee for a July 2015 grant falls due around now, and Google Patents lists no foreign family members, which suggests (unconfirmed) US-only protection. Second, the patent could lapse if the fee goes unpaid, as happened to ADRess and the MERL NMF patent. Northwestern's licensing listing makes a licence a realistic, bounded-cost option. The architecture should keep the repetition kernel behind a clean interface so that the product can ship with it licensed, disabled for US customers, or replaced by a self-similarity or HPSS-only fallback. Other gaps remain open: iZotope, Sony, Dolby, DTS, Yamaha and Audionamix portfolios were not searched, and post-2015 classical-DSP filings were not systematically reviewed. Keep a dated design log that cites the pre-filing papers each component implements, write every line in-house, and never copy GPL reference code into the closed product.

## Conclusion

The shift in thinking is from *masks* to *models*. PHARMAKON's current splitter makes four independent masking decisions and so cannot be mixture-consistent, stereo-preserving or instrument-aware. A KAM-style architecture makes each stem a small, testable model of "what this source looks like elsewhere in time and frequency", and lets the Wiener filter resolve conflicts optimally. The product's tempo grid and kick detector are worth more in that framework than in any published classical system, because they supply exactly the side information (repetition period, transient positions) that those systems had to estimate blindly. That is the one structural advantage a classical PHARMAKON has over generic benchmarks. It is also why the expected gains on this material should exceed what DSD100 numbers suggest, though they will not reach neural quality.

Two decisions should come before more algorithm work. First, measure the oracle IRM2/MWF ceiling on the ground-truth stems, so that every later change is judged against what masking at those resolutions can reach rather than against the current baseline. Second, settle the US 9,093,056 question (claims, maintenance-fee status, licence quote) before repetition modelling becomes load-bearing in shipped code. The algorithm that fits loop music best is also the one with an active patent behind it, and keeping it swappable is cheaper than redesigning later.
